How to Use Spec Automation
Part 1 is the N/S standard general-requirements section.

Start with the built-in N/S wording and review every project-specific fill-in before issuing the specification. Save a named template only when you intentionally create an approved reusable variation.

Recommended four-step workflow

  1. 1. Project. Enter project information and complete the bracketed fields in Part 1, Part 2, and Part 3. Each Part has an Improve with AI button for reviewed writing assistance.
  2. 2. Sources + Local AI. Add manuals, drawings, submittals, specifications, datasheets, Word files, or text files. Use Analyze for fast text extraction or Analyze with AI for drawings, scans, schedules, tables, and difficult pages.
  3. 3. Source Review. Verify every equipment item, fill-in, clause, source citation, and suggested destination. Accept only information confirmed against the source.
  4. 4. Review & Export. Resolve missing fill-ins and review warnings, confirm the complete Part 1–3 specification, and export the automatically numbered Word or PDF file.
Using Improve with AI

Describe the writing change in the chat-style request box and select the arrow or press Enter. Shift+Enter adds a new line. Review the GitHub-style red and green comparison, choose Accept or Revert for each change, then select Accept Changes. The three most recent revisions remain in Writing History.

Fillable fields stay protected

Bracketed fields such as [EQUIPMENT TYPE] and [SYSTEM NAME / MODEL] remain available after AI writing changes. Their removal is locked unless the request explicitly names the placeholder and asks to remove it.

What Local AI does

Qwen3-VL reads each selected source page for mechanical, electrical, plumbing, controls, equipment, part numbers, ratings, dimensions, utilities, and specification wording. Results are marked for review and never enter the issued specification until a user accepts them.

If Local AI will not start

Confirm you are signed in with the shared database login. If Ollama is not installed on the approved company AI computer, use the official Ollama for Windows download and ask the automation administrator to install the approved Qwen3-VL model. If the model is still loading, wait a minute and try the source again.

Helpful controls

Reanalyze refreshes extraction from a saved source. Reload Starter refreshes standard wording while preserving approved equipment, accepted source wording, and completed fill-ins. In text editors, use Tab or Shift+Tab to change hierarchy and Enter to continue numbering.

Approval and fill-ins perform different jobs.

Equipment approval builds the Article 2.2 equipment list. Project and technical fill-ins complete bracketed requirements. Source suggestions add reviewed equipment-specific wording to the selected article.

What belongs in each part?

Part 1 — General

Repeated administrative requirements: scope, related work, quality assurance, submittals, delivery, approved-equal procedure, closeout documents, and standard warranty requirements.

Part 2 — Products

The equipment itself: system type, performance, dimensions, utilities, construction, brushes, pumps, electrical controls, safety devices, water/reclaim systems, components, and options.

Part 3 — Execution

What must happen at the site: preparation, installation, plumbing and electrical connections, startup, commissioning, testing, training, O&M delivery, and final acceptance.

Example wording

N/S setup examples: Use these examples to identify where information belongs. Replace them with the standard N/S wording and the requirements for the current project.

Part 1 example — General 
PART 1 - GENERAL

1.1 SUMMARY
A. Provide a complete automatic vehicle-washing system, including equipment, controls, accessories, startup, testing, and training required for an operational installation.

1.2 SUBMITTALS
A. Submit product data, equipment layouts, utility requirements, wiring information, and installation instructions.
B. Clearly identify proposed deviations from the specification.

1.3 QUALITY ASSURANCE
A. Equipment shall be furnished by a manufacturer regularly engaged in producing vehicle-washing systems of the type specified.

1.4 DELIVERY, STORAGE, AND HANDLING
A. Protect equipment from damage, moisture, contamination, and improper storage before installation.

1.5 CLOSEOUT SUBMITTALS
A. Provide operating instructions, maintenance information, final drawings, parts information, and warranty documentation.
Part 2 example — Products 
PART 2 - PRODUCTS

2.1 SYSTEM DESCRIPTION
A. Provide an automatic vehicle-washing system configured for the vehicle sizes and operating conditions indicated for the project.

2.2 PERFORMANCE REQUIREMENTS
A. System shall accommodate vehicles up to [MAXIMUM HEIGHT] high and [MAXIMUM WIDTH] wide.
B. Wash cycle shall include the selected wash, rinse, wheel-wash, and optional reclaim functions.

2.3 CONSTRUCTION
A. Structural components exposed to the wash environment shall be corrosion resistant and suitable for continuous wet operation.

2.4 ELECTRICAL AND CONTROLS
A. Provide a control system consisting of [CONTROL PANEL / PLC / HMI DESCRIPTION].
B. Provide automatic and manual operation, status indication, alarms, emergency-stop monitoring, and required safety interlocks.

2.5 UTILITIES
A. Electrical service: [VOLTAGE], [PHASE], [AMPERAGE].
B. Water service: [CONNECTION SIZE], [PRESSURE], [FLOW].
Part 3 example — Execution 
PART 3 - EXECUTION

3.1 EXAMINATION
A. Verify that the wash bay, foundations, utilities, drainage, and access conditions are ready for equipment installation.

3.2 INSTALLATION
A. Install equipment according to approved drawings and manufacturer instructions.
B. Coordinate field plumbing and electrical connections with the responsible contractors.

3.3 STARTUP AND TESTING
A. Perform startup, adjustment, and functional testing of the complete system.
B. Demonstrate required operating sequences and safety devices.

3.4 TRAINING
A. Train the owner’s personnel in normal operation, shutdown, safety, and routine maintenance.

3.5 CLOSEOUT
A. Deliver final O&M manuals, drawings, warranties, and service contact information.
Do not paste an entire O&M manual into these boxes.

Use only requirements that should become part of the project specification. Leave out serial numbers, troubleshooting histories, spare-parts ordering instructions, prices, and maintenance procedures that apply only after installation.
